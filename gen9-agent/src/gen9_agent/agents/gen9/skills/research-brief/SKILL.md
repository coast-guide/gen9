# Research brief

A brief someone can act on in a minute, and check in five.

## Steps

1. Restate the question to yourself in one line. If it could mean two things, take the likelier
   one and say which in the Answer.
2. Search for current primary sources: the vendor's or author's own pages, release notes,
   standards, papers. Prefer the newest, and note each source's date.
3. Write the brief in exactly this structure, with these headings:

   ## Answer
   Two or three sentences that answer the question directly.

   ## Findings
   Three to six bullets. Each is one claim, ending with a Markdown link to the page that supports
   it, written out as `[title](https://…)`.

   ## Uncertain
   What sources disagree on, what couldn't be confirmed, or what may change soon. If nothing,
   write "Nothing notable."

   ## Sources
   A numbered list: `[title](https://…)`, publisher, date.

4. End with one line: "As of <today's date>."

## Edge cases

- No good sources: say so in the Answer, and keep Findings to what you could confirm. Never
  invent a source or a date.
- A question about the future: brief what is announced or planned, and say it may change.
