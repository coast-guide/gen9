# Gen9 and the EU AI Act's transparency rules (Art. 50)

What Gen9 does about the AI Act's transparency obligations, which apply from 2 August 2026, and
what is left to whoever runs it. Read from:
- Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Art. 50;
- the Commission's FAQ on Art. 50 (updated 24 July 2026);
- its guidelines on the transparency obligations (20 July 2026, cited by paragraph, ¶);
- the Code of Practice on Transparency of AI-generated Content (10 June 2026).

Not legal advice: a record of how Gen9 reads them, to be checked by whoever deploys it.

## Who is who

- **Whoever runs Gen9** puts an AI system into service, so they are its provider. Gen9 is free
  and open-source software, but Art. 2(12) doesn't lift Art. 50 from such systems.
- **The companies behind its models** (gen9-models: OpenAI's and DeepSeek's, through OpenRouter)
  provide general-purpose AI models. Art. 50 doesn't bind a model as such, but the guidelines
  encourage marking at the model level (¶27).

## Telling people they're talking to an AI (Art. 50(1) and (5))

People are told "at the latest at the time of the first interaction", and not only in terms and
conditions or with "generic references to 'assistant'" (¶38). "A single, prominent notification
before the first interaction" near the input usually suffices (¶37, ¶40).

- **The web app:** under the composer, from before the first question: "Gen9 is an AI system and
  can be wrong. Check its work before you rely on it."
- **The terminal:** `gen9 login` prints the same line after "Signed in as …".
- **The agent itself:** its instructions say it is an AI system, not a person, and that it says so
  when asked (gen9-agent's `agents/gen9/AGENTS.md`).
- **The notification email:** it holds no generated text, only that a task ran and a link, so it
  carries no label (an email an AI agent generates would carry "an AI label at the top", ¶36).
- **When the agent writes to other people** through a connector (an email, a message, a post, a
  comment), it starts with one line saying an AI system wrote it and on whose behalf ("Written by
  Gen9, an AI system, on behalf of …"). The guidelines ask agents to disclose "both their
  artificial nature and the person on whose behalf they are acting" (¶31), such as "an email
  generated by an AI agent … that features an AI label at the top" (¶36). It leaves the line out
  only for words the person wrote, or when they ask. The runs don't carry the person's name,
  which would be sent to the model provider on every call just for this, so the line names them
  when the conversation does, else "the sender". A message sent from their own account shows
  them anyway.

## Marking what Gen9 generates (Art. 50(2))

Generated audio, images, video and text are marked "in a machine-readable format". Out of scope:
- an agent's intermediate steps and actions not meant to be perceived, such as a web request
  (¶63);
- content from "simple data processing", such as a rendered frame (¶64).

- **What Gen9 generates.**
  - Answers (text), and the files its agent makes in a chat's environment.
  - Not images, audio or video: gen9-models defines `image`, `speak` and `transcribe`, but Gen9
    uses none of them.
- **Answers as they are shown.** Free-form text "cannot transport metadata", and the Code's
  measure for it is a watermark embedded as the text is generated (Sub-measure 1.1.2), which only
  the model can do. A system's provider "may rely on the marking solution implemented by an
  upstream model provider" (¶74). Gen9 passes the models' text through as they wrote it. Whether
  each provider marks its text is theirs to say, and is the operator's to check.
- **Wherever Gen9 gives out a message or a file** (its API, and the export a person downloads),
  each carries `"ai_generated"`:
  - `true` for Gen9's answers and the files it made (`"origin": "output"`);
  - `false` for what the person wrote or attached.

  The export's README says what the mark means. The Code asks signatories for signed metadata in
  containers (Sub-measure 1.1.1). Gen9's mark is plain JSON, not signed.
- **Timing.** A system on the market before 2 August 2026 has until 2 December 2026 for the marking
  (the FAQ).

## Left to whoever runs Gen9

- **Art. 50(4):** text generated with Gen9 and published "on matters of public interest" is
  labelled by whoever publishes it. So are deep fakes, which Gen9 doesn't make.
- **The models' own marking:** choose, and check, what the model providers do (above).
- **Keep the disclosure:** a changed web app or terminal keeps Art. 50(1)'s notice before the first
  question.
